ОПИСАНИЕ (копировать одним блоком)
There is a patch of grass in downtown Miami with a two-thousand-year-old circle cut into the bedrock underneath it. A few blocks away, round houses are carved into the limestone under a cinema. Just west of Brickell Avenue, more than a million artifacts have come out of the ground since the early 2020s, ahead of a tower.

This is not a lost-civilisation mystery. The Tequesta held the mouth of the Miami River for roughly two thousand years, and almost everything we know about them was found because a developer needed a permit. A slow, evidence-first walk through the archaeology under a modern American city — no jump scares, built to be left on in the dark.

A general-interest historical investigation. Dates for the Miami Circle are given as a range (commonly cited as roughly 1,700-2,000 years for the radiocarbon material, with occupation at Brickell Point stretching further back); figures for artifact counts, site extents and population estimates follow published reporting and excavation summaries and are treated as estimates, not settled numbers. The 1896 mound account rests on workers' later recollections. The Tequesta are not ancestral to the Seminole or Miccosukee nations of Florida today.

ПОСТ В СООБЩЕСТВО

There is a lawn in downtown Miami, about the size of a tennis court, with a two-thousand-year-old circle cut into the bedrock underneath it. Twenty-four basins, thirty-eight feet across, carved by hand at the mouth of the river. The state paid twenty-six million dollars for that patch of ground in 1999 — and then covered the circle back up to protect it. Most people who walk across it have no idea. Tonight's episode goes down through the layers under one square mile of a modern American city, and what is down there is not a mystery. It is a town that was never lost.
