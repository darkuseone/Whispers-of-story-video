## DESCRIPTION
Long before 1776, the land that became the United States was already mapped, farmed, governed, and remembered — just not in the kind of book most of us were handed in school. This is a slow walk through the records that actually survived: Ice Age footprints in New Mexico, canals under Phoenix, Spanish letters from towns that later vanished, winter counts painted on hide, and maps that were crowded with nations until someone filed them under someone else's name. Settle in — this one is built to be listened to with the lights low.

Notes
A note on sources: the White Sands dates were disputed after 2021 because aquatic seeds can take up old carbon; the 2023 study added terrestrial pollen and luminescence ages in the same range, which is how the trackway is treated here — as strong, still-discussed evidence, not as a closed origin story for every people on the continent. Wampum belts and the Great Law of Peace survive as living tradition plus later transcriptions, not as a single 16th-century signed manuscript. Disease mortality is described without a single percentage because published estimates disagree. This episode is not a survey of every nation, and it does not speak for any nation.
